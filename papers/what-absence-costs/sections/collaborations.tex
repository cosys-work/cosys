\section{Monotone, composable collaborations}\label{sec:collab}

The setting is a collaboration without a clerk. Each person or organization keeps its own log of
signed records. Records are only ever added: a correction is a new record that points at the one it
corrects, and a withdrawal is a new record too. Records travel between parties as messages, at any
speed and in any order, and there is no shared ledger in the middle and no clerk whose copy counts as
the copy. A digital signature does two jobs at once. It names who stood behind a record, and it binds
them to its exact content, so that changing a single character makes a different record they never
signed. Anyone who holds a record and the signer's public key can check both without asking anyone.

\citet{karna2026} gives this setting a precise form. Each party acts through a box, a Mealy machine
whose inputs and outputs are signed records and whose state is a fold over its log. A collaboration
wires existing boxes together through mailboxes, and the result is again a box, so collaborations
nest: a team that forms a team adds one more box and a few wires, and the boxes already there do not
change. Nothing outside the boxes and the wires coordinates them, and the model has no central
coordinator. On this view an orchestrator, where one is useful, takes part as one more party, with its
own log and its own signature.

A box is monotone when more records never take away an output it has already produced. The CALM
theorem, for Consistency As Logical Monotonicity, says when a distributed program can give consistent
answers without its machines stopping to agree. \citet{hellerstein2010} conjectured it,
\citet{ameloot2013} proved it, in a revised form, for networks of relational transducers, and \citet{hellerstein2020}
give the general account: a program has a consistent implementation without coordination exactly
when it is monotone. Their own illustration is garbage collection, where a machine may discard an
object only when no other machine still refers to it, and the only way to know it has heard from
everyone is to ask them all and wait. \citet{karna2026} proves its own form of the result for boxes over signed
records, with the proofs checked in Lean 4 \citep{karna2026proofs}. When every box is monotone, the logs and views of a
collaboration do not depend on the order or duplication of deliveries. A step that depends on a
record being absent can come out differently under two fair delivery orders, and the model asks for a
coordinating signature at that step, such as a co-signed receipt or a witnessed timestamp.

Trust in this setting is indexed by its observer. No view of the shared record exists apart from
someone holding it. Each observer ranks what it holds inside its own fiber of trust, in the model's
terms a fibration over observers, so parties holding the same signed records can rank them
differently without any ranking being forced on another, and no number about a person is published
for everyone.

Meaning is kept the same way. Each party keeps an olog \citep{spivak2012}, a small categorical
diagram of what its own records mean to it. Where parties need to share terms, they grow an ontolog
of declared equivalences between their ologs, each added by co-signature and each carrying a
round-trip law: a record exported into the other party's vocabulary and imported back comes out
unchanged. A lab's ``result'' and a clinic's ``report'' become the same thing only when both have
signed that they are.

Almost every move in such a collaboration is monotone: a proposal, a comment, a reading, a vote, a
co-signature, each an addition that leaves the rest in place. A few moves depend on something being
absent, such as an approval that requires no open objection, a revocation, or a slot that can be
taken once. Those few are the subject of this paper.
